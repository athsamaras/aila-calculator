Enter the values available for the patient. The calculator uses the largest GRIXA model that the data allow and reports a risk group and the predicted risk of death at five time horizons.

About GRIXA

GRIXA (Graded Risk model, Internationally validated, eXplainable All-cause mortality prediction) comprises three nested models of death from any cause following hospitalisation in a cardiology department, built from routinely collected data. They use progressively broader predictor sets, from admission (GRIXA-Core) to discharge (GRIXA-Dis) and with further biomarkers (GRIXA-Bio).

How the risk is calculated

Each version of GRIXA is a penalised Cox regression model. The calculator uses the largest version that the entered data allow.

linear predictor = Σ coefficient × (value − mean) / SD
risk(t) = 1 − S₀(t)exp(linear predictor + local shift)

Coefficients, means, SDs, and baseline survival S₀(t) are those of the publication and were fixed before validation. The local shift is one number per time horizon that adapts the baseline risk to the chosen setting; it is zero for Greece.

Risk group and predicted probability

The risk group (low, intermediate, or high) is defined by thirds of the linear predictor in the development cohort. It depends only on the ranking of patients, which was consistent across the validated settings.

The predicted probability also depends on the baseline risk, which differed between settings. The calculator therefore asks for the setting first. Where no baseline exists, it reports the risk group and the range of probabilities across the validated settings.

Limits

  • For adults discharged alive from a cardiology department. Not for children, and not designed for patients in intensive care at the time of calculation.
  • In trial populations with acute heart failure, ranking was weaker (C-index 0.63–0.66); a heart failure model is preferable there.
  • Every item is needed for the version in use. The published validation also imputed missing values, which this calculator does not do.
  • Baselines for London, Amsterdam, and Hamburg were estimated in the validation cohorts themselves and have not yet been tested in new patients.
  • The estimate describes a group of similar patients and cannot say what will happen to one person. Contributions shown for each item are associations and do not show what would happen if a value were changed.

Privacy

All calculations run in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent or stored. Do not enter names or other directly identifying information.

Evidence

GRIXA was developed in Greece and then tested, without refitting, in cohorts that differed in country, care setting, case mix, and baseline risk.

15 737patients in the development cohort (two hospitals in Northern Greece, 2010–23)
37 690patients in six validation cohorts with follow-up (6471 deaths)
80 583hospitalisations in 14 US hospitals, tested for death in hospital

Main findings

Discrimination

The ranking of patients was consistent in inpatients and outpatients from four countries (C-index of GRIXA-Core 0.74–0.77), from 90 days to 5 years. In two acute heart failure trials it was lower (0.63–0.66), as expected from their narrow case mix.

Calibration

Baseline risk was lower outside Greece. After one baseline parameter per horizon was updated, the average difference between predicted and observed risk was 2 percentage points or less in every cohort.

Algorithms

Each model was fitted with penalised Cox regression, gradient boosting, and random survival forests. In validation, machine learning did not outperform Cox regression, which this calculator uses.

Publication

The manuscript describing the development and international validation of GRIXA is in preparation. The development cohort comes from the CardioMining registry (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05176769).
